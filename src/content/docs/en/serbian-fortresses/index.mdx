In Serbia there are dozens of fortifications from different periods. Rather than trying to visit them all from a single list, choose a fortress as part of a specific itinerary along the Danube or through Novi Sad, Niš, Pirot or Raška.

The National Tourism Organisation of Serbia lists Bač, Belgrade Fortress, Diana, Fetislam, Golubac, Maglič, Niš, Petrovaradin, Pirot, Ram, Smederevo, Stari Ras and Zvečan among its principal sites.[^1]

## Along the Danube

### Golubac

Golubac is a 14th-century medieval fortress on the right bank of the Danube. The official tourism portal identifies the operator, `Tvrđava Golubački grad`, and publishes its contact details.[^2]

This is more than a freely accessible ruin: before travelling, check the fortress's official website for current opening hours, tickets, visitor zones and restrictions. Combine it with Đerdap National Park, bearing in mind that individual park trails have their own access rules.

### Smederevo

Smederevo Fortress was built in the 15th century under Đurađ Branković and is divided into the Small Town and the Great Town.[^3]

It stands within Smederevo itself and is suitable for a separate urban trip without complicated mountain logistics. Check access to individual parts of the complex and event arrangements before visiting.

### Ram

Ram lies on the right bank of the Danube in the municipality of Veliko Gradište. The National Tourism Organisation describes it as the first typical military artillery fortification on this stretch of the river.[^1]

Include Ram in a Danube itinerary rather than making it your only destination on a trip from Belgrade.

## Novi Sad and Belgrade

### Petrovaradin Fortress

Petrovaradin Fortress was built from the late 17th to the late 18th century and forms part of the urban fabric of Novi Sad.[^4]

On a first visit, explore the upper fortress and viewpoints. Check museum visits, underground galleries and special tours separately: free access to the surrounding public space does not mean unrestricted access to every interior.

### Belgrade Fortress

Belgrade Fortress and Kalemegdan stand at the confluence of the Sava and Danube. The outdoor grounds fit naturally into a walk through the city centre, while museums, towers and exhibitions have their own visiting arrangements.

## Southern and western Serbia

### Niš Fortress

Niš Fortress is a well-preserved 18th-century Ottoman fortification on the bank of the Nišava in central Niš.[^1] Combine it with a day exploring the city centre rather than travelling solely to see its walls.

### Pirot Kale Fortress

Kale Fortress lies in the urban centre of Pirot.[^1] It fits well into a trip using Pirot as a base for Zavojsko Lake or Stara Planina.

### Maglič

Maglič stands on a rocky ridge above the Ibar near Kraljevo.[^1] Before travelling, check actual access and the condition of the approach: transport and pedestrian access to such sites change more readily than their historical background.

## Stari Ras

Stari Ras is a 12th-century fortification at Gradina near Novi Pazar. Together with Sopoćani and other regional monuments, it forms part of a World Heritage site.[^1]

Use the dedicated [Stari Ras, Novi Pazar and Pešter itinerary](/lifestyle/travel/stari-ras-novi-pazar/) (Russian) for this part of the country.

## Planning fortress visits

<Steps>
1. Choose a region rather than a random fortress from the general list.
2. Check whether the site is an equipped visitor attraction, an urban public space or archaeological ruins.
3. For paid attractions, visit the operator's official website immediately before travelling.
4. For remote ruins, check roads, parking, the walking approach and weather.
5. Do not assume free access to outdoor grounds guarantees access to towers, museums and underground areas.
6. Combine the fortress with a nearby town or nature itinerary so that the trip consists of more than the journey there and back.
</Steps>
