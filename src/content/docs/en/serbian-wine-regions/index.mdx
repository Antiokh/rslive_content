Plan a wine trip in Serbia around **the region and transport**, rather than a random list of wineries. The National Tourism Organisation of Serbia identifies Vojvodina, Šumadija, the Morava Valley and Negotinska Krajina as wine-growing destinations; more detailed itineraries feature Srem and Fruška Gora, Župa and Toplica, Niš and Knjaževac, and other areas.[^1]

<Aside type="caution" title="Do not combine wine tasting with driving">
  If travelling by car, designate a driver who will not drink alcohol. Alternatives include a taxi, transfer, organised tour or overnight stay in the region. Do not count on tasting small quantities and then driving.
</Aside>

## Choosing a region

| Region | Where to go | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Srem and Fruška Gora** | Sremski Karlovci, Irig and the slopes of Fruška Gora | Riesling, Tamjanika, regional wines; Bermet, a traditional aromatised wine |
| **Šumadija** | Topola and surroundings | Modern wineries, Prokupac and Tamjanika alongside international varieties |
| **Župa and the Morava Valley** | Aleksandrovac and central Serbia | Prokupac, Tamjanika and regional white wines |
| **Negotinska Krajina** | Negotin, Rajačke and Rogljevačke pimnice | Tamjanika, Prokupac, regional red wines |
| **Danube region and Smederevo** | Smederevo and the Danube Valley | Smederevka and the Danube wine route |

## Grape varieties featured in official tourist itineraries

The National Tourism Organisation of Serbia highlights **Prokupac, Tamjanika, Vranac and Smederevka** as local and traditional varieties.[^2]

This is a guide for visitors rather than an ampelographic register. For a specific bottle, check the grape varieties on its label and with the producer.

### Prokupac

A red grape variety closely associated with central and southern Serbia. For a trip centred on Prokupac, look towards Šumadija, Župa and other central regions rather than a random northern winery.[^2]

### Tamjanika

This aromatic white variety features widely in tourism descriptions of Šumadija, the Morava Valley and the Negotin region.[^1][^2]

### Smederevka

Smederevka is associated primarily with the Smederevo area. The National Tourism Organisation includes it in the Danube wine route and identifies it as characteristic of the area.[^3]

### Bermet

`Bermet` is **not a grape variety** but an aromatised wine historically associated with Sremski Karlovci and Fruška Gora. The official tourism portal lists it among the characteristic wines of Srem.[^3]

## Srem and Fruška Gora

Sremski Karlovci makes a convenient first wine day in Srem. The town, Stražilovo and wineries are covered in the dedicated [Fruška Gora and Sremski Karlovci itinerary](/lifestyle/travel/fruska-gora-sremski-karlovci/) (Russian).

Do not turn up at a private wine cellar without checking first. The Tourism Organisation of Sremski Karlovci publishes a directory of wineries with contact details and information on tastings and cellar visits; arrange the time directly with your chosen winery.[^4]

## Šumadija

Šumadija is one of the major wine-growing areas of central Serbia. Use Topola and its surroundings as your starting point, but choose a particular winery through official regional itineraries and its own contact information.[^1]

Do not attempt to visit many wineries in one day: even two pre-arranged tastings require time for travel, tours and meals.

## Župa and the Morava Valley

The Aleksandrovac/Župa area is suitable for a trip focusing on Prokupac and Tamjanika. The National Tourism Organisation identifies Župa and Toplica as a separate wine destination and the Morava Valley as a major wine-growing region.[^1]

If wine is the focus of the trip, stay overnight in the area or arrange a transfer: distances between wineries make travelling on foot from one tasting to another unrealistic.

## Negotinska Krajina

The Negotin area combines wineries with historic `pimnice`, complexes of wine cellars. The National Tourism Organisation associates the region with Tamjanika, Prokupac and a distinct Danubian winemaking tradition.[^1][^3]

Before visiting Rajačke or Rogljevačke pimnice, check which premises actually welcome visitors: a historic cellar on the map does not necessarily have an operating tasting room.

## Booking a tasting

<Steps>
1. Choose a region and arrange a way back without driving after drinking.
2. Find a particular winery on the official regional tourism portal or through the local tourism organisation.
3. Open the winery's own website or contact it directly.
4. Confirm the date, time, number of guests, tour languages, cost and any included food.
5. Ask whether an advance payment is required and whether wine can be purchased on site.
6. If travelling by car, agree before the tasting who will remain sober and drive.
</Steps>

When buying wine as a souvenir, check the rules on bringing alcohol into the destination country; the fact that you bought it at a tasting does not determine the customs allowance.
