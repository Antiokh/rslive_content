The **National Council of the Russian National Minority** (*Nacionalni savet ruske nacionalne manjine*) is a body of cultural self-government of the Russian national minority within Serbia's legal system. It is not a private association, a representative office of the Russian Federation or a diplomatic institution. The council has legal personality and exercises public powers delegated by Serbian law in four areas: culture, education, information, and official use of language and script.[^1][^2]

It should be distinguished from [KSORS](/en/russians-in-serbia/ksors/). KSORS coordinates independent compatriot organisations, whereas the National Council is elected under Serbian law and forms part of the general system of national minority councils.

## Legal status and powers

The Serbian Constitution allows members of national minorities to elect national councils in order to exercise collective rights and cultural self-government in culture, education, information and official use of language and script.[^2] The Law on National Councils of National Minorities defines their detailed powers, election procedure, governing bodies and financing.[^3]

Within those statutory limits, a national council may among other things:

- adopt its statute, financial plan and reports;
- establish institutions and organisations in its areas of competence;
- participate in the governance of institutions of particular importance to the minority;
- submit opinions and proposals to public authorities;
- participate in matters concerning curricula, textbooks and education in the minority language;
- support media and information in the Russian language;
- participate in decisions concerning official use of Russian language and script;
- cooperate with Serbian authorities, other national councils and organisations abroad.[^3]

<Aside type="note" title="Who the council represents">
  In legal terms, the council represents the Russian national minority in the fields specified by law. It is not a parliament of all Russian citizens in Serbia, does not automatically represent recent temporary residents, and does not decide immigration, citizenship, tax or consular matters.
</Aside>

## Establishment and elections

The first council was formed after the 2018 national minority council elections. The second convocation was elected in the general national minority council elections on 13 November 2022, with mandates allocated by the Republic Electoral Commission.[^4]

National council members are elected either directly or through an electoral assembly, depending on the number of registered voters belonging to the minority. Participation is tied not to Russian citizenship but to membership of the Russian national minority and entry in Serbia's special electoral roll.[^5]

The post-2022 immigration wave therefore overlaps only partly with the Russian national minority as a Serbian constitutional-law category. A foreign national does not acquire voting rights for the council merely by obtaining temporary residence in Serbia.

## Composition and leadership

According to the council's official information in August 2026, **Milovan Ćurčić** is president, **Larisa Milić** secretary, and **Arsen Plahin** and **Dragan Logvinov** vice-presidents. The executive committee is headed by **Žana Knežević**, with **Konstantin Orlov** as deputy.[^6]

The second convocation has 15 members. The membership list is published on the council's website and in its meeting records.[^1]

The headquarters are at Braće Jovanovića 24, Pančevo. The main email is `kontakt@nsrnm.org`; official council documents also list `065 2960 450`.[^7]

## How the council works

The council has committees corresponding to the four statutory areas:

- culture;
- education;
- information;
- official use of Russian language and script.

In practice, its work includes competitions and cultural programmes, preparation and distribution of teaching materials, cooperation with schools and libraries, support for community projects and public information for members of the minority.[^8]

The council also cooperates with Serbia's Ministry for Human and Minority Rights and Social Dialogue, provincial and local authorities, Russian institutions, Russian House and civic associations.[^9]

## Main projects

### Russian language and education

Language preservation and education are among the council's most visible activities. In 2026 it ran the **“Living Russian Word”** programme, prepared a workbook for learning Russian and organised activities in several Serbian towns.[^8]

The council has statutory consultative and participatory powers in matters of curricula and textbooks affecting the minority. It does not, however, control all Russian-language teaching in Serbia and does not independently issue Serbian state diplomas.

### Cultural competitions and festivals

The council supports literary, musical and visual-art projects, exhibitions, concerts, children's competitions and programmes relating to Russian and Serbian-Russian culture. Financial or informational support for an event does not by itself mean that the council is its sole organiser.[^10]

### Census and visibility of the minority

Before the 2022 Census the council informed residents about their right to voluntarily declare Russian ethnicity.[^11] The census recorded **10,486 residents of Serbia who declared Russian ethnicity**.[^12]

That figure is not the number of Russian citizens living in Serbia. The census uses usual residence and voluntary ethnic self-identification, while migration records are organised by citizenship and immigration status.

## Financing and reporting

Serbian law provides for national councils to be financed from the budgets of the Republic, the autonomous province and local governments, as well as donations and other lawful income.[^3]

Republic-level funding is allocated according to statutory criteria combining an equal share for registered councils with factors such as the size of the minority, the number of institutions and the scale of activities.[^3]

The council publishes individual decisions, competitions, meeting records and other documents, but they do not form a single complete archive of all financial and project reporting. To verify a specific grant or project, check the funding decision, contract, organiser's report and the relevant budget record.

## Relations with Russia and political interpretations

Serbian law expressly permits national councils to cooperate with organisations and institutions in the minority's state of origin.[^1][^3] Contacts with the Russian Embassy, Russian House, cultural institutions and foundations are therefore not by themselves outside the normal national-minority framework.

At the same time, the National Council is part of Serbia's public-law system: its legal status, elections, powers and basic public financing are defined by Serbia. Describing it simply as a “Russian influence organisation” is legally imprecise. The opposite claim — that a national council is politically neutral by definition — would also be too broad. Political positions and controversial activities should be attributed to specific decisions, persons, partners and projects.

## When the council is relevant

The council is relevant to questions concerning:

- Russian-language and cultural programmes;
- educational initiatives and teaching materials;
- support for projects of the Russian national minority;
- information in Russian;
- collective cultural and language rights;
- contacts with Serbian authorities in those fields.

Russian passports, Russian citizenship and consular acts belong to the Russian Embassy. Temporary residence, permanent residence and Serbian citizenship are handled by the competent Serbian authorities. The National Council does not replace any of those procedures.
