Serbian statistics describe several different populations that are often mixed together in public discussion. The most important distinction is between **citizenship-based migration statistics** and **ethnicity recorded by the population census**.

At the end of 2025, Russian citizens were the largest group of foreign nationals with valid temporary residence in Serbia: **54,917 people**, or **47.7%** of all 115,093 foreigners with valid temporary residence.[^1] Russian citizens were also the largest group of permanently resident foreigners: **7,032 people**, or **35.9%** of the total 19,612.[^1]

These numbers do not mean that there were exactly 61,949 “Russians in Serbia”. The two residence categories are distinct administrative stocks, while citizens who acquired Serbian citizenship, short-term visitors, people who declared Russian ethnicity and Russian-speaking citizens of other states are measured differently.

In 2025 the stock of temporary residents continued to grow, but much more slowly than in 2022–2024, while the number of permanent residents increased sharply. The timing is consistent with part of the post-2022 population reaching the three-year residence threshold introduced by the amended Foreigners Act, but this is an interpretation of the timing rather than a published individual-level causal analysis.[^1]

## First temporary-residence permits in 2025

Serbia issued **32,922** first temporary-residence permits in 2025. Russian citizens received **9,003**, or **27.3%** of the total.[^1]

For all nationalities combined, employment was the most common basis for a first permit in 2025, accounting for **67%**. Russian citizens were also the largest nationality among first family-reunification permits (**3,654**, 55.5% of that category) and first study-related permits (**1,056**, 55.2%).[^1]

The report states that the displayed 2025 residence data were generated on **25 February 2026**. This matters when comparing figures from different official publications.[^1]

## Census: Russian ethnicity is a different measure

According to Serbia's 2022 census, **10,486 residents declared Russian ethnicity**.[^3] This figure should not be compared directly with the number of Russian citizens holding residence permits. The census is based on usual residence and voluntary ethnic self-identification, whereas migration statistics are administrative data organised by citizenship and immigration status.

A Serbian citizen of Russian ancestry may appear in the census category but not in foreigner-residence statistics. Conversely, a Russian citizen with temporary residence may identify with another ethnicity or may not fall within the census's usual-residence population.

## How to use the numbers

- Do not add temporary residence, permanent residence, citizenship and census ethnicity and call the result “the number of Russians”.
- Check whether an official table describes a **stock at a given date** or **permits issued during a year**.
- Keep first permits separate from renewals and from all valid permits.
- Check the report date and methodological notes before comparing years: Serbia's migration-law and reporting framework changed substantially in 2023–2024.
