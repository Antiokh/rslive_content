RSLive is a practical encyclopedia about relocation, documents, institutions and everyday life in Serbia. Most of the encyclopedia is written in Russian; the English edition is a **selective edition**, not a machine-translated copy of the whole site.

It focuses on material that is useful on its own in English: information about RSLive, procedures that can be shared with an English-speaking landlord or other counterpart, and reference articles explaining Russian migration and Russian organisations in Serbia.

### Hosting tourists in your own property

Serbia has a special regime for a **private individual (`fizičko lice`)** who provides short-term tourist accommodation in an apartment, room or house (`domaća radinost`) or in a rural tourist household. The host must be the owner or co-owner of the property; a member of the owner's household may provide the service with the owner's written consent. The statutory limit is up to **30 individual beds and no more than 30 guests at the same time**.[^1]

For an apartment, room, house or rural tourist household, categorisation is handled by the local self-government unit (`JLS`) through the state **eTurista** system. The host's eTurista account is opened through the JLS. Once the accommodation is operating legally, guests are registered and checked out through eTurista; the system also contains the data fields used for foreign guests and can generate a confirmation of stay after registration.[^1]

A private host also has separate annual obligations for the lump-sum tourist tax (`boravišna taksa`) and tax on income from accommodation services. For private individuals, the tourist tax is paid by the host in the amount set by the competent authority; it is not charged to the guest as a separate line in the same way as by companies and entrepreneurs.[^1]

<Aside type="caution" title="For foreign property owners">
  The public eTurista FAQ describes the private-host regime and ownership requirements but does not publish a complete separate onboarding procedure for a foreign owner. Before listing the property on Airbnb, Booking or another platform, ask the JLS where the property is located which identifiers and documents it requires for your eTurista profile and tax decisions. Ownership of the apartment alone is not the final step.
</Aside>

Do not confuse commercial tourist accommodation with ordinary registration of a foreigner staying at a private address. The [eUprava landlord guide](/en/foreigner-residence-registration/) explains the latter procedure; a categorised accommodation provider must follow the eTurista regime for its guests.[^1]
