RSLive, the “Guide to Serbia”, is an open practical knowledge base about relocation, documents, work, business and everyday life in Serbia.

We started the project in October 2024 as a wiki. The goal was straightforward: collect useful information that was scattered across chats, personal guides, spreadsheets and official websites, and update it in one place instead of creating another parallel guide.

Since then we have changed the engine, separated editorial content from the technical code, added automated checks, offline access and separate language editions. The core principle remains the same: knowledge should accumulate, be checked and be corrected while preserving its revision history.

## How it began: not another guide

The first recorded page of what became RSLive was created on **3 October 2024**. The old DokuWiki log preserves the creation of the start page with the note “created, structure from ChatGPT”.[^1] From the beginning we used AI to help organise material, while the factual basis came from documents, accumulated experience and community sources.

On **13 October 2024**, a separate page titled “Why another guide?” recorded the original idea behind the project.[^1]

By October 2024 there was already a large amount of information about life in Serbia: Telegram chats and channels, personal guides, blogs, spreadsheets, translations of rules, government websites and user experience. The main problem was not scarcity. The information was scattered and became outdated at different speeds.

A personal guide records the situation at the time of publication. Later a law changes, a bank updates its requirements, an office moves or a link stops working. The old post continues to circulate, and correcting it may be impossible. Several versions of the same instruction then coexist.

We chose a wiki so that information could be corrected in the same place while previous versions remained in the history. Related subjects could be linked, repeated information could be consolidated into tables, and a small error could be fixed without rewriting the entire guide.

The archived page stated the purpose directly: **“The accumulation and preservation of knowledge is the sole purpose of this wiki.”**[^1]

The same page named two common sources of stress in emigration: “You know nothing” and “You know nobody”. We tried to reduce the first by organising information and the second by involving people willing to share experience.[^1]

RSLive was not designed as a personal blog. The early plan already included section curators, fact-checking, cross-links, new sources and continuous updates to published material.[^1]

## DokuWiki: the first version of the site

We chose **DokuWiki** as the first engine. It provided the core wiki functions immediately: page creation and editing, internal links and revision history. That allowed us to collect material before building a custom publishing system.

During autumn 2024 we added plugins for tables, presentation, approval workflows, address data and other tasks. On **5 November 2024**, RSLive opened a public DokuWiki bug report because the installation running on `rslive.ru` could not contact the plugin repository.[^2] The issue confirms that RSLive was already operating as a DokuWiki site and extending the standard system.

In January 2025 we moved the project to a new server. The technical Git history records **6 January 2025** with the commit message `new server! happy new year! temporary dokuwiki-test for combo plugin test`.[^3] The editorial corpus itself remained in place.

At the same time we were moving material from thematic communities and personal notes into the common wiki. On **14 April 2025**, the author of the Telegram channel “Птичьи перелеты: Сербия” publicly wrote that she had joined “the project of a common WIKI about life in Serbia” and was moving her articles into RSLive.[^4]

This was the original model in practice: collect material from different authors in one shared base where it could continue to be checked, corrected and expanded.

## Why we replaced DokuWiki

As the corpus grew, the site depended increasingly on a live server and a collection of plugins. Tables, collapsible blocks, maps, forms, navigation and approval tools worked as separate extensions that had to be maintained together.

At the same time we needed a better mobile interface, fast search, stable URLs, previews for messaging apps, more capable maps and tables, language editions and offline access.

We decided to preserve the wiki model while replacing its technical implementation. For us, the wiki model means editable pages, revision history, linked material and collaborative work. It does not require one specific engine.

## Spring 2026: migration to Astro and Starlight

The major migration began on **30 April 2026**. The Git history shows us preparing the move, importing the DokuWiki corpus into MDX, configuring the new site, restoring required interface behaviour, checking old syntax and adding redirects from legacy addresses.[^5]

The new foundation became **Astro and Starlight**, with publication through **Cloudflare Pages**. Most pages are now built in advance and published as ready files. This reduced dependence on one server having to assemble each page at request time.

We did not migrate text alone. The old articles already contained tables, steps, notes, collapsible blocks, maps, images, internal links and specialised elements. The replacement pages had to preserve those practical functions.

We also preserved legacy URLs. RSLive links were already present in chats, bookmarks and other guides, so redirects were added for old routes.[^5]

The active DokuWiki was removed from the new engine in early May. We kept the old corpus as a historical archive and used it to check the completeness of the migration.[^5]

## 22 May 2026: a separate public content repository

On **22 May 2026**, we created the public `Antiokh/rslive_content` repository and moved the editorial corpus out of the site's technical repository.[^6]

After that, content and engine development became separate. Articles and editorial data live in the public repository. Astro, components, builds, search, maps and other implementation details remain in `Antiokh/rslive.ru`.

A text correction can now be proposed without server access. GitHub shows the diff, the author of the change, review comments and the version that is ultimately accepted.

A public RSLive announcement described the workflow directly: GitHub can be used to correct errors, update instructions, add material and submit changes for review; after acceptance, the site rebuilds automatically.[^7]

The contribution workflow changed, but the original principle of a shared and correctable knowledge base remained.

## Migration review after launch

Launching the new site did not finish the migration. We continued comparing the new corpus with the old one to find missing details, broken links and over-aggressive reductions.

On **23 August 2026**, one such review of the old childbirth guide restored practical shopping categories and shops that had been partially lost during the original migration.[^8]

At the same time we formalised an editorial rule: the absence of an official source does not automatically make a practical observation useless. When information helps with preparation and does not contradict established facts, we preserve it as institutional practice or user experience and keep it separate from law and official procedure.[^9]

By late August the historical DokuWiki corpus had been moved out of the site's working tree into a separate project archive. We use it to check provenance and recover useful details that may have disappeared in later edits.[^5]

## Offline access, language editions and an open licence

On **6 May 2026**, we added PWA support so RSLive could be installed as a web application. During August we expanded offline support into a complete local copy of first-party material. On **16 August 2026**, the project gained a complete first-party offline precache; later changes also included first-party map data in the offline copy.[^10]

The offline copy makes it possible to save instructions in advance and open them without a network connection, for example while travelling, at an airport or inside an office.

During summer 2026 we also began developing **selective English and Serbian editions**. We do not automatically translate the entire Russian encyclopedia. We create a separate language page when the material needs to work independently in that language. The English “About RSLive” and Serbian “О RSLive” pages were added on **8 August 2026**.[^11]

On **23 August 2026**, we relicensed the editorial content under **Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0)**.[^12] The licence allows covered text to be reused and adapted while preserving attribution and the ShareAlike terms.

## What changed, and what did not

In less than two years we changed servers and engines, separated content from code, and added automated checks, language editions, an updates log, offline access and first-party map data. The main project rules remained stable.

- **Accumulate instead of starting over.** When we update material, we preserve useful information and correct what has become outdated.
- **Correct errors openly.** We do not treat an article as a final version; we review corrections and preserve revision history.
- **Separate levels of evidence.** We describe law, official procedure, institutional practice and user experience separately.
- **Treat the engine as a tool.** DokuWiki helped us start quickly; Astro, Starlight, GitHub and offline support became necessary as the corpus and site requirements grew.

- **14 April 2025:** a public post records an independent author moving her material into RSLive.[^4]
- **30 April to early May 2026:** we move the main corpus from DokuWiki to Astro/Starlight and Cloudflare Pages and restore required functions and legacy routes.[^5]

## Editorial principles

- **Verifiability.** We check laws, official government websites and other primary sources for legal, tax and administrative claims.
- **Facts are separated from experience.** We identify legal rules, official procedures, institutional practice and personal experience as separate levels of information.
- **Practical value is preserved.** We do not remove a useful sequence of actions or dated user experience only because an official instruction does not mention it; we identify its evidential level instead.
- **Currency.** We show the latest substantive change date and recheck time-sensitive information when editing.
- **Correctability.** We preserve revision history. If you find an error, report it or propose a correction.
- **Openness.** We publish the encyclopedia's texts in a [public repository](https://github.com/Antiokh/rslive_content) and distribute covered editorial content under CC BY-SA 4.0.

## Who is responsible for the articles

When an article has a named author, the author's name is displayed with the publication and included in structured metadata. For encyclopedic pages without personal authorship, the responsible publisher is the RSLive editorial project.

An author profile identifies editorial authorship or contribution. It does not replace advice from a licensed lawyer, accountant, doctor or other regulated professional when such advice is required.

During substantive review, current law and current primary sources take priority over an archived RSLive version. We use the archive to check provenance and preserve useful practical details.

## How to propose a change

- Open the relevant page and use its edit link.
- [Create an issue](https://github.com/Antiokh/rslive_content/issues/new) if you find an error or outdated information.
- Provide a primary source where possible and explain which statement it confirms or contradicts.
- If you are reporting personal experience, include the place and approximate date so that one case is not presented as a universal rule.
