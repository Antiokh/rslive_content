RSLive, the “Guide to Serbia”, is an open practical knowledge base about relocation, documents, work, business and everyday life in Serbia.

RSLive began as a way to collect useful information that had been scattered across chats, personal guides, spreadsheets and official websites. The site and editing process changed over time, but the purpose remained the same: accumulate knowledge, verify it, correct errors and preserve the history of changes.

## How it began: not another guide

By the time RSLive appeared, there was already plenty of information about life in Serbia. The problem was not scarcity but fragmentation and different rates of obsolescence.

A personal guide records the situation at the time it is published. Later a law changes, a bank updates its requirements, an office moves or a link stops working. The old text can continue circulating even when it can no longer be corrected.

A wiki made it possible to update information in one place, connect related material and preserve earlier versions. The archived project page stated the purpose directly: **“The accumulation and preservation of knowledge is the sole purpose of this wiki.”**[^1]

The same archive records two common problems of emigration: “You know nothing” and “You know nobody”. The first is addressed by a shared knowledge base; the second by people willing to contribute experience. RSLive was therefore conceived as a continuously updated encyclopedia rather than a personal blog.

## DokuWiki: the first version of the site

The first technical foundation was **DokuWiki**. It provided pages, internal links, editing and revision history without requiring a separate publishing system to be built first.

As the project grew, tables, presentation tools and other functions were added. Material from thematic communities and personal notes also moved into the shared knowledge base.[^1][^2]

This established the main model of the project: useful information is collected in one place, then checked, corrected and expanded.

## Why DokuWiki was replaced

As the encyclopedia grew, the site needed a better mobile experience, fast search, stable links, maps, interactive elements, language editions and offline access.

DokuWiki served the first stage well, but further development became more difficult. The wiki principle itself did not depend on one engine: editable pages, revision history, linked material and collaborative work remained the important parts.

## Moving to a new engine

The encyclopedia moved to **Astro and Starlight**. The migration preserved not only text but also the practical functions of the articles: tables, step-by-step instructions, notes, maps, images and interactive elements.[^3]

Old page addresses were preserved as well. RSLive links already appeared in chats, bookmarks and other material, so a technical move was not supposed to break them.

The former DokuWiki was kept as an archive. It remains useful for checking the completeness of the migration and the origin of older material.[^3]

## Open editing

Project texts are open to proposed changes through GitHub.[^4] Before publication it is possible to see what is changing and what comments appeared during review. Accepted corrections are then published on the site.

The editing method changed, while the principle of a shared and correctable knowledge base remained.

## Reviewing the migration

Moving to a new site did not automatically finish the migration. The old and new corpora continue to be compared to find missing details, broken links and excessive reductions.

Such checks have already restored practical information that was partly lost during the original migration.[^5]

The editorial rule is simple: the absence of an official source does not automatically make a useful observation worthless. Institutional practice and user experience are preserved when they help with preparation and do not contradict established facts, but they remain separate from law and official procedure.[^5]

## Offline access, language editions and an open licence

RSLive can be installed as a web application, and material can be saved in advance for use without a network connection.[^6]

The English and Serbian editions are developed selectively. They are standalone materials rather than automatic translations of the entire Russian encyclopedia.[^7]

Editorial content is distributed under **Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0)**. The licence permits reuse and adaptation of covered text while preserving attribution and the same ShareAlike terms for derivative work.[^8]

## What changed, and what did not

The engine, editing method and available site features have changed over the life of the project. The main rules have remained stable.

- **Knowledge accumulates.** Useful information is preserved during updates, while outdated information is corrected.
- **Errors are corrected openly.** Revision history remains available for review.
- **Levels of evidence stay separate.** Law, official procedure, institutional practice and user experience are described separately.
- **Technology remains a tool.** It changes when that helps store, check and publish knowledge more effectively.

## Editorial principles

- **Verifiability.** Laws, official government websites and other primary sources take priority for legal, tax and administrative claims.
- **Facts are separated from experience.** Legal rules, official procedures, institutional practice and personal experience are identified as different levels of information.
- **Practical value is preserved.** A useful sequence of actions or dated user experience is not removed only because an official instruction does not mention it; its evidential level is identified instead.
- **Currency.** Time-sensitive information is rechecked during substantive editing.
- **Correctability.** Revision history is preserved. If you find an error, report it or propose a correction.
- **Openness.** Project texts are open for review and proposed changes and are distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0.

## Who is responsible for the articles

When a person's name is shown with a publication, it identifies an editorial contribution to that material. For encyclopedic pages without a personal name, the responsible publisher is the RSLive editorial project.

A listed name does not replace advice from a licensed lawyer, accountant, doctor or other regulated professional when such advice is required.

During substantive review, current law and current primary sources take priority over an archived RSLive version. The archive is used to verify provenance and avoid losing useful practical details.

## How to propose a change

- Open the relevant page and use its edit link.
- [Create an issue](https://github.com/Antiokh/rslive_content/issues/new) if you find an error or outdated information.
- Provide a primary source where possible and explain which statement it confirms or contradicts.
- If you are reporting personal experience, include the place and approximate date so that one case is not presented as a universal rule.
